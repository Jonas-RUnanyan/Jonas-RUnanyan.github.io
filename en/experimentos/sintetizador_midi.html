The project

I did this project during "Fast and Fourier-rous: Master the speed of sound," a 9-day BEST Course at INSA Lyon (France) on real-time audio processing and embedded systems. BEST (Board of European Students of Technology) is an international non-profit organization that runs intensive technical courses across Europe; admission is selective (via a motivation letter), and it brings together engineering students from dozens of different countries, living together on campus throughout the course. I paired up with a classmate from another country for the final project: a polyphonic MIDI synthesizer (4 voices) on a Teensy board, with code-generated synthesis (no samples) and a Python control app, presented in a live demo on the final day alongside the other teams.

The system

Synthesis engine and firmware (C++)
I wrote MyDsp, a class that extends AudioStream from the Teensy Audio Library and generates every audio sample by hand: four waveforms, an attack/release envelope, vibrato, pitch bend and clipping distortion. The drums don't use samples either: each instrument (kick, snare, hi-hat, tom, cowbell) is a combination of oscillators and noise with different exponential envelopes. The firmware assigns the 4 voices to incoming MIDI notes, reads the volume and pitch bend potentiometers, and exposes a small serial protocol so an external app can trigger percussion and change parameters in real time.

Control application (Python)
My teammate built a Tkinter desktop app that connects to the Teensy over serial, automatically detecting the port. It shows the active notes of the 4 voices on an LCD-style screen and provides live control over waveform, distortion, attack/release, drum pads and a metronome with several modes, running on its own thread so it doesn't block reading the serial port.

Current status

Functional: polyphonic synthesis, drums, metronome, hardware control and the real-time Python app. As documented, what remains pending is note visualization with pitch shifting and finer attack/release behavior.

What I learned

  • Real-time audio programming on a microcontroller, generating every sample within the time budget of an audio block.
  • Designing code-based percussion synthesis (no samples), combining oscillators and noise with per-instrument envelopes.
  • Teamwork under time pressure (a full project in just a few days) with a teammate from another country, in English.
  • Experience in an international environment: selection via motivation letter, living alongside engineering students from across Europe, and presenting results in a live demo.
