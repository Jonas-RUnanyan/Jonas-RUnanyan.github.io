The project

This Bachelor's Thesis (Computer Science Engineering degree, UPM) investigates the application of interactive genetic algorithms to procedural city generation in Minecraft, in the context of the international Generative Design in Minecraft Competition (GDMC). The goal is to study how bringing the user into the evolutionary loop makes it possible to balance creative control, structural diversity, and functional coherence against the fully automatic approaches the competition requires.

The system

The program analyzes an unknown terrain and builds a complete city on top of it by combining several procedural generation techniques. Below is a summary of the system's main modules.

Terrain analysis and urban layout
The system analyzes the terrain received through the GDMC-HTTP API, places the building plots, and computes the path network connecting them using an ACO-Steiner algorithm (ant colony optimization applied to the rectilinear Steiner tree problem), complemented with A* for individual route planning and subsequent post-processing and decoration.

Shape grammar for the buildings
I improved upon a shape grammar engine from a previous work that builds buildings through recursive volume subdivision, with probabilistic parameters controlled by the evolutionary genome, along with building-type-specific grammars and an integrated material palette system.

Interactive genetic algorithm (IGA)
The core of the thesis: an interactive genetic algorithm that optimizes the city's aesthetic aspects through indirect parametric encoding. The user guides the evolution by periodically comparing pairs of candidate cities, and the system alternates that input with phases of guided exploration, taking steps to avoid premature convergence and mitigate evaluator fatigue.

User study

I designed and ran a user study to evaluate the interactive genetic algorithm against random generation, measuring aesthetic, functional, and adaptive aspects, as well as overall rating and choice time across generations. The results show that the IGA produces cities with higher aesthetic ratings than the random ones, although without conclusive statistical significance; users perceived that their decisions influenced the system's evolution, prioritizing color harmony as the main selection criterion, and a progressive increase in evaluator fatigue was observed.

What I took away

  • Designing and implementing an interactive genetic algorithm with indirect parametric encoding for a subjective aesthetic problem.
  • Building a custom shape grammar engine for procedural building generation in a voxel environment.
  • Applying ant colony optimization (ACO-Steiner) and A* to urban path network planning.
  • Designing and running a user study, analyzing quantitative and qualitative results (including evaluator fatigue in HITL algorithms).
  • Researching and writing a full scientific thesis of over 100 pages, including related work, results discussion, and future work.
